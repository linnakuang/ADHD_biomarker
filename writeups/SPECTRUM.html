Spectrum cohort report — the case for inclusion, methods, and findings (S1–S8)

What this is. A single self-contained report on the "spectrum" analyses that test whether including the 1,503 currently-excluded neurodevelopmental children (573 ADHD-without-ASD + 930 other-NDD) lets us learn more about ASD. It folds three previously-separate documents:

  • Part I — the collaborator-facing case for inclusion (formerly RESULTS_Spectrum.md).
  • Parts II–VI — the methods-and-evidence report for S1–S5 (formerly SPECTRUM_REPORT.md).
  • Part VII — S6–S8: per-condition specificity, cross-group contrast, and developmental timing (formerly GRADIENT_VS_STEP.md).

All analyses run at /gus_analysis/; outputs in results/. Last updated 2026-09-18. Companion: RESEARCH_AGENDA.md (the B-I…B-V / M-I…M-IV follow-on list and status); Track-A/B writeups TRACK_A.md, TRACK_B.md.

Governing framing — "both framings, let the data decide." Throughout, we do not pre-commit to "ASD is special" or "ASD is just the far end of a shared continuum." For every question and every condition we compute the honest evidence for both and let each finding fall where the data puts it. The excluded NDD/ADHD children are what make the intermediate comparisons observable at all.

Part I — The case for including the NDD/ADHD groups

Question. The study currently analyses 145 ASD children against 1,851 NT and excludes 1,503 children with other neurodevelopmental diagnoses (573 ADHD-without-ASD, 930 other-NDD) by design. Does bringing those children in let us learn more about ASD? Five analyses (S1–S5), all on the paper's baseline / 51 conditions / §2 rules; only the pool learned from changes.

question verdict
S1 Do residualized axes get more reproducible with n? Yes, decisively. K=3 bootstrap cosine 0.82 (a component below the §2 floor at n=145) → 0.97 at n=1,648. n, not biology, was the binding constraint.
S2 Does ASD stand out from ADHD/other-NDD in the shared latent space? Real but modest. ASD-vs-other AUC 0.587 (perm p=0.006), barely above burden, less distinct than ADHD-vs-other (0.638). A graded tilt (toward the psychiatric axis), not an island.
S3 Does inclusion lift the temporal density ceiling? Yes, for estimability. Thin slices (the A1/A2 artifact driver) 53%→12%; full 6-window 0–18 goes 1/6 → 6/6 windows passing §2.
S4 …does that make a temporal factorization valid? No. CP is stable on the spectrum (worst component 0.65→0.93) but the temporal factor still tracks the density envelope (|r|≈0.88, unmoved by 10× data). Estimable ≠ valid. Strengthens A1: the temporal artifact is structural, not small-sample.
S5 Can we borrow power for ASD-specific inference? Yes — the payoff. Decomposing each condition into shared-NDD + ASD-specific, with shrinkage: 7–11/51 conditions are genuinely ASD-specific (epilepsy, sleep, congenital anomalies, movement/behavioral disorders ↑; headache/pain ↓), while the largest ASD-vs-NT signals (anxiety, refractive, otitis, obesity) are 70–104% generic NDD — unknowable without the excluded groups.

One-line case for collaborators: inclusion removes the power constraint that leaves the headline model under its own stability floor (S1); the temporal-trajectory ambition is a dead end at any n and should be dropped (S3/S4); and the excluded children convert "ASD differs from typical kids" into the sharper, honest, ASD-specific finding of which conditions truly distinguish ASD from the rest of the neurodevelopmental spectrum (S2/S5/S6/S7). Keeps the ASD focus.

What this means for the collaborator case — the inclusion argument is stronger for being honest:

  1. Power is the binding constraint, and inclusion removes it (S1). The paper's headline K=3 model is under-powered at n=145 — one axis fails the team's own §2 floor. The excluded children fix that directly (0.82 → 0.97 cosine; failing component 0.76 → 0.96). Every downstream axis-based claim rests on a firmer footing.
  2. Inclusion lets us ask a question the current design cannot (S2). "Does ASD comorbidity differ from other neurodevelopmental conditions?" is unanswerable against an NT-only comparator — residualizing vs NT makes NT trivial by construction. The excluded groups are the matched-utilization, non-ASD controls that turn "ASD differs from typical children" (partly a burden/ascertainment artifact — see Track-A A3b/A5b and Track-B B2) into "ASD differs from ADHD and other NDD in these specific ways."
  3. The specificity answer is itself a defensible, publishable result. ASD sits at AUC ≈ 0.59 (p = 0.006) from other-NDD, mostly a psychiatric-axis tilt, less distinct than ADHD. "ASD is a graded position within a shared NDD comorbidity landscape, not a categorical comorbidity signature" is a real finding — obtainable only with these groups. S5–S7 then sharpen it to a short, mechanistically-interpretable core.

The framing the collaborators worried about is preserved: this keeps the ASD focus (ASD is the group we characterise within the spectrum); it is not a pivot to an NDD-wide paper.

Part II — Cohort, features, and the shared residualization scheme

Cohort (Boston Birth Cohort, postnatal EHR). 3,499 children with a clean 4-way label from the priority rule ASD > ADHD > other-NDD > NT:

label n role
NT 1,851 baseline / expected-rate reference (rung 0)
Other NDD 930 currently excluded — part of "the rest" (rung 1)
ADHD (no ASD) 573 currently excluded — part of "the rest", ASD's nearest neighbour (rung 2)
ASD 145 the group of interest (rung 3)

Derived pools used throughout: analytic cohort = 1,996 (145 ASD + 1,851 NT, the paper's design); spectrum pool = 1,648 (ASD + ADHD + other-NDD); non-ASD-NDD "rest" = 1,503 (the excluded children). Label overlap is real and reported: 61/145 ASD also carry an ADHD flag, 140/145 carry ≥1 other-NDD flag (the priority rule assigns the primary label).

Feature space. The paper's fixed 51 retained conditions (nmf_kept_conditions.txt).

Residualization vs NT (shared across all analyses). For each condition we subtract a single, fixed NT-expected-rate vector from every child of every group. That vector is NT prevalence per condition, directly standardized over sex × is_black × education (target = full-cohort distribution; strata with <5 NT pooled). Because the same NT vector is subtracted from all groups, the resulting latent space is comparable across ASD / ADHD / other-NDD. For matrix analyses the residual is clipped at 0 and rows are unit-L2-normalized (identical to the paper's build_residual_matrix / normalize_for_nmf, except the NT rate is computed once and shared).

You asked (about S2): how was the data residualized, and against what baseline? The procedure above is used everywhere. We build one NT-expected-rate vector by direct standardization — within each stratum of (sex × is_black × education) we take the NT prevalence of each condition, then average those stratum rates using the whole cohort's stratum weights (strata with < 5 NT pooled). A child's residual is their raw condition vector minus this same vector, clipped at 0. Because the identical vector is subtracted from every child regardless of group, all groups land in one comparable space, and NT becomes ~0 by construction (it is excluded from the latent fit and appears only for context).

Fidelity check (why the comparison is valid). With this pipeline the ASD-only K=3 bootstrap reproduces the paper's published numbers to the third decimal (cosine 0.819 / concordance 0.675 vs 0.80 / 0.66). So differences downstream are attributable to who is in the pool, not to a re-parameterization.

Pre-registered §2 stability rules (unchanged from the paper): bootstrap matched cosine ≥ 0.80; dominant-assignment concordance ≥ 0.60; density floor 5 children per cell; window-keep if ≥60% of conditions clear the floor; density-artifact guard |r(temporal factor, per-window density)| > 0.7; "null is publishable."

Part IV — Spectrum analyses S1–S5 (methods & evidence)

S1 — do residualized axes get more reproducible with n?

Method. s1_axis_robustness.py. Fit K=3 NMF (sklearn mu/Frobenius, best of restarts) on the shared NT-residual. Run the full §2 bootstrap (B=200 resamples × 3 restarts): resample children, refit, Hungarian-match components to the reference (scipy linear_sum_assignment), report mean matched cosine, weakest matched component, and dominant-assignment concordance. Compare two fits — ASD-only (n=145, the paper's model) vs full spectrum (n=1,648) — and trace a learning curve by refitting on random spectrum subsamples of increasing size.

K=3 object boot cosine worst component concordance
ASD-only (n=145) 0.819 0.764 ✗ below floor 0.675
Full spectrum (n=1,648) 0.970 0.960 0.867

The paper's own K=3 model sits on the §2 floor and its weakest axis (0.764) is below it — one of the three axes is not reproducible at n=145. Learning curve (K=3 cosine): 0.803 → 0.849 → 0.880 → 0.906 → 0.939 → 0.969, monotone. K=2 is already stable at 145 (cos ~0.89), so the gain is specific to the K the paper reports.

Verdict. Strong yes. Reproducibility is limited by n, not biology; inclusion turns floor-scraping axes into rock-solid ones. Figure: results/s1_learning_curve.pdf.

S2 — does ASD stand out in the shared latent space?

Method. s2_asd_separation.py. Learn K=3 on the full spectrum residual, then test whether ASD children are distinguishable from other groups with a 5-fold CV ROC-AUC (logistic + random forest, clf_auc). Every contrast is disciplined against a burden-only baseline (total code count) and an ADHD-vs-other-NDD calibration (are two non-ASD NDD groups even separable?). Significance by label permutation (perm_null, N=500). Sweep K=2..8.

contrast latent W (K=3) raw 51-residual burden only
ASD vs other-NDD (the specificity test) 0.587 (perm p=0.006) 0.624 0.566
ASD vs ADHD 0.522 0.588 0.474
ASD vs ADHD + other 0.541 0.620 0.539
ADHD vs other-NDD (calibration) 0.638 0.636 0.569

Real but weak: ASD-vs-other is statistically significant yet barely beats burden (0.587 vs 0.566), the raw residual beats the compressed latent (0.624 > 0.587, so K=3 loses ASD info), and ADHD is more distinct from other-NDD (0.638) than ASD is — ASD is not the stand-out group. K-sweep flat (0.57–0.60). Where ASD does tilt (per-axis ASD − other-NDD): away from axis0 (generic somatic/sensory, −0.084) and toward axis2 (psychiatric: anxiety/mood, +0.084).

Verdict. ASD is a modest, statistically-real tilt within a shared NDD comorbidity space — not a distinct island, and less distinct than ADHD. Figures: results/s2_embedding.pdf, tables s2_separation_auc.csv, s2_axis_group_means.csv.

S4 — is the temporal factorization actually valid on the spectrum?

Method. s4_spectrum_tensor.py. Run the paper's exact nonnegative CP/PARAFAC (multiplicative updates, kernel copied verbatim from a1) on both pools, two binnings, rank scan R∈{2,3,4}, with the same bootstrap stability and the same density-artifact guard |r(temporal factor C, per-window density)| > 0.7. Reproduces A1's failure on ASD-only as a check.

R=3, 6w ASD (145) spectrum (1,648)
thin slices 53% 12%
bootstrap cosine 0.820 0.957
worst component 0.648 ✗ 0.933
concordance 0.688 0.853
|r(temporal, density)| 0.887 ✗ 0.880 ✗

Two results, reported together: (1) estimability/stability are fixed by inclusion (worst component 0.648 → 0.933; same at dx-anchored 3w, 0.803 → 0.960). (2) Validity is not — the density-artifact guard fails at every pool × scheme × rank (|r| = 0.82–0.99), essentially unmoved by 10× the data (0.887 → 0.880). Explained variance stays ~0.15. Reason: the per-window density envelope shape (pediatric codes cluster in early childhood) is n-invariant — more children crush thin slices but don't change when codes occur.

Verdict. Inclusion converts an unestimable temporal model into a stably estimable but still artifactual one. This strengthens A1: the temporal-factor-as-artifact is structural, not small-sample. Constructive corollary: the failure is confined to the temporal (C) mode — the condition (B) mode is highly reproducible, so stable co-occurrence is learnable, developmental timing is not (without a per-child utilization denominator). Table: results/s4_spectrum_tensor.csv. S8 (Part VII) is the direct follow-up that tries three ways to remove this artifact.

S5 — hierarchical shrinkage for ASD-specific inference (the payoff)

Method. s5_hierarchical_shrinkage.py. Decompose the elevation the study reports, per condition:

e_ASD(j) = s(j) + δ(j)
(p_ASD − NT) = (p_rest − NT, shared NDD) + (p_ASD − p_rest, ASD-specific)

s(j) is pinned by the 1,503 excluded + 1,851 NT (tight); δ(j) is noisy (145 vs 1,503) and is stabilized by empirical-Bayes shrinkage across the 51 conditions (DerSimonian–Laird τ², normal-normal posterior).

You asked (about S5): explain the shrinkage top-level, and — since every formula was a point estimate — how are the confidence intervals actually generated? Four steps:

  1. Decompose the elevation: e_ASD = (p_rest − p_NT) + δ. Shrinkage acts only on the ASD-specific piece δ.
  2. Estimate genuine between-condition spread. Treat the 51 δ's as noisy draws around a global mean μ with true spread τ. The DerSimonian–Laird estimator (standard random-effects meta-analysis) recovers τ² by method of moments: it takes the observed spread of the δ's (Cochran's Q) and subtracts the part explained by each δ's own sampling noise SE², leaving τ² as the "real" variance. (Here μ = +0.012, τ = 0.033; SE_j = √[p_asd(1−p_asd)/145 + p_rest(1−p_rest)/1503].)
  3. Shrink each δ. The shrinkage factor B = τ² / (τ² + SE²) is the fraction of a condition's variance that is signal: B ≈ 1 (tight, barely moved), B ≈ 0 (noisy, pulled to μ). The shrunk estimate is δ_shrunk = μ + B·(δ − μ).
  4. The interval — the step you flagged. Shrinkage also sharpens uncertainty. The posterior standard deviation is post_sd = √(B · SE²) (smaller than the raw SE, because borrowing adds information). The interval is δ_shrunk ± z · post_sd, z = 1.645 (90%) or 1.96 (95%). So the CIs come from the posterior SD, not the raw SE. A condition "survives" when its whole interval excludes 0.

Findings. Global ASD-vs-rest shift μ = +0.012 (tiny on average), but real between-condition structure τ = 0.033 (Q = 117.7, df 50, p ≪ 0.001). Shrinkage: mean |δ| 0.040 → 0.022. 7/51 survive at 95%, 11/51 at 90% — the aggregate S2 signal was weak because it is concentrated, not diffuse.

You asked: I saw 5 conditions in the table but you said 7 — which are they? A threshold difference. 7 survive at 95%; 11 at 90%; the earlier table showed the top 5 by magnitude. S5's survivor set (below) is the 90% set; S6/S7 build on it.

Genuinely ASD-specific (90% CI excludes 0): excess — sleep +0.055, behavioral +0.052, epilepsy +0.051, movement +0.045, congenital digestive +0.044, congenital musculoskeletal +0.041, personality +0.038, diabetes +0.030, glaucoma +0.029; deficit — headache/migraine −0.049, pain −0.032. The epilepsy + congenital-anomaly excesses converge with Track B (B1).

Part V — What changes with the expanded cohort (feasibility & opportunity)

The two-part takeaway. (1) Feasibility — almost everything limited by sample size at n=145 is unlocked: axis robustness (S1), temporal estimability (S3), co-occurrence structure (S4 condition mode), stable per-condition prevalence (S5). The one thing inclusion does not rescue is temporal validity (S4/S8): that ceiling is structural, and more data proves it rather than fixing it. (2) Opportunity — inclusion makes two genuinely new question-classes askable, both keeping the ASD focus: how does ASD differ from other NDD (S2), and which specific comorbidities are truly ASD-specific vs shared NDD (S5–S7). Neither is possible against an NT-only comparator.

Part VI — Honest caveats (S1–S5)

  • Label overlap from the priority rule: ASD-vs-other contrasts are between primary-label groups with shared underlying flags; if anything this understates a "pure" ASD contrast.
  • Residualizing vs NT makes NT trivial by construction — NT is excluded from the latent fit (its residual ≈ 0) and appears only for context in the embedding.
  • Burden confound persists (ASD carries more codes); we report the burden baseline beside every contrast rather than adjusting it away (burden is part of the phenotype).
  • Ascertainment still differs across groups — no per-child utilization denominator (the Track-A A3b ceiling). Coding rate cannot be divided out; a modest AUC could be attenuated real signal or residual ascertainment.
  • Robustness ≠ validity (S1): the respiratory/atopic axis is the same likely artifact Track B flagged — more data makes it more stably estimated, not more real.
  • S5 δ is not further demographically adjusted (NT standardization cancels; both groups are NDD so are more comparable than vs NT); single τ² across conditions; shrinkage is the multiplicity control (the 7 at 95% are the conservative set), not FDR.

Part VII — Per-condition specificity, cross-group contrast, and developmental timing (S6–S8)

S1–S5 established that ASD's comorbidity signal is concentrated in a specific set (S5). The three follow-ups here sharpen that set from three angles. Each reuses the Part II residualization and, for S6/S7, the Part IV/S5 shrinkage machinery verbatim; the definitions below are only the pieces new to S6–S8.

Definitions used in S6–S8. - Elevation vs NT e_g = p_g − p_NT; ASD-specific deviation δ = p_ASD − p_rest (as in S5). - Shared fraction shared_frac = (p_rest − p_NT) / (p_ASD − p_NT) — the fraction of ASD's total elevation over NT the rest of the spectrum already accounts for. ≈0 → abrupt step; ≈0.5 → gradient; ≳0.85 → generic NDD. - Cochran–Armitage trend z — linear-trend test across the ordered groups (scores NT=0, other-NDD=1, ADHD=2, ASD=3). The four raw prevalences are always shown alongside so the true shape is visible. - Three cross-group contrasts (S7), each with its own DerSimonian–Laird τ² (identical to S5): delta_vs_rest = p_ASD − p_rest (= S5); delta_vs_ADHD = p_ASD − p_ADHD (the hard test — ASD vs its nearest neighbour, isolating autism from attention); delta_ADHD_vs_other = p_ADHD − p_other (calibration). - CP / PARAFAC + guards (S8) — nonnegative CP factorizes the child × window × condition tensor into R rank-1 triples (child-loading, condition vector, temporal factor). A component is real only if it is stable (bootstrap cosine ≥0.80, concordance ≥0.60), artifact-free (|r(temporal factor, per-window density envelope)| ≤ 0.7), and group-separating (best per-component ASD-vs-rest child-loading ROC-AUC ≥ 0.60).

S6 — per-condition specificity: gradient vs. step

s6_gradient_vs_step.py. Survival (from S5's shrunk δ) is checked first, so an ASD-specific signal is never hidden by a coincidentally small e_ASD. Six shape categories: STEP (δ survives, δ>0, shared_frac<0.5 — ASD-specific excess jump), STEP-DOWN (δ survives, δ<0 — ASD-specific deficit), GRADIENT+ (δ survives, δ>0, shared_frac≥0.5 — graded continuum ASD is boosted atop), SHARED (δ n.s., |e_ASD|≥0.03, shared_frac≥0.5 — generic-NDD gradient), ASD-LEAN (δ n.s., |e_ASD|≥0.03, shared_frac<0.5), FLAT (δ n.s. and |e_ASD|<0.03).

S7 — cross-group contrast: is ASD specific even against ADHD?

s7_cross_group_contrast.py. S6 asks whether ASD differs from the pooled rest; pooling can manufacture apparent specificity (a condition common in both ASD and ADHD but rare in other-NDD looks "ASD-specific vs rest" only because other-NDD dilutes the pool). S7 asks the harder question: does that specificity survive when the comparator is ADHD alone?

Label counts (all 51): AUTISM-SPECIFIC 2 · SHARED-WITH-ADHD 7 · ADHD-PEAK 4 · ASD-DEFICIT 0 · SHARED-NDD 36 · FLAT 2. (τ: vs-rest 0.033, vs-ADHD 0.035, ADHD-vs-other 0.030 — the calibration τ is non-zero, so ADHD and other-NDD are themselves per-condition distinguishable.)

S8 (M-II) — developmental timing: can a temporal signal survive ascertainment?

s8_temporal_normalized.py. S4 showed a plain CP on the spectrum tensor is estimable but invalid (temporal factor tracks the density envelope). S8 attacks that artifact three ways, all built around it, and scores each vs the S4 baseline on all four criteria (estimable / stable / artifact-free / group-separating).

You flagged the ascertainment artifact yourself. S8 is built entirely around it: all three methods are attempts to remove it, and the density guard is the arbiter. A method that is stable and artifact-free but does not separate groups is a clean negative, itself a publishable methods result.

Approach What it changes Result
BASELINE S4's absolute-age 6-window residual tensor reproduces the artifact
(a) REL-TIMING each event placed in a quartile of that child's own first→last-code span — removes the population envelope by construction artifact persists
(b) DENS-EQUAL each age-slice rescaled to equal total mass, so no factor can be driven by high-volume windows artifact persists
(c) UTIL-NORM each cell = the condition's share of that child's coding in the window (count ÷ child's total events), NT-residualized — divides out coding volume cell-by-cell de-confounds, but no ASD signal

Part VIII — Unified interpretation and S6–S8 caveats

Read together, the eight analyses give a consistent, honest picture of where ASD's comorbidity signal lives:

  • It is marginal, not temporal. All the signal is in which conditions are elevated (rates), none in when they appear (timing). S8 is a clean negative — useful, because it closes off the temporal-modeling direction rather than leaving it an open promissory note. This is S4 confirmed and extended.
  • The specific signal is small and shrinks under scrutiny. ASD-specific vs the pooled rest = 11 conditions (S5/S6), overwhelmingly step-shaped. ASD-specific vs ADHD alone = 2 (epilepsy, glaucoma; S7). Each tightening of the comparator removes conditions, exactly as expected if much of ASD's profile is shared neurodevelopmental liability.
  • The largest elevations are shared — and often peak in ADHD. Anxiety, mood, and the whole somatic/sensory cluster are ASD's biggest raw elevations but are spectrum-wide; anxiety and mood actually crest at ADHD.

For the inclusion case this is the payoff argued in Part I: the excluded children let a single undifferentiated "elevated in ASD" list be split into (a) a short, mechanistically-interpretable autism-specific core (epilepsy above all), (b) a broad shared neurodevelopmental gradient, and (c) a definitive statement that timing adds nothing — none of which the ASD-vs-NT design can produce. Both framings held open throughout are true, for different conditions.

A classification nuance worth knowing (S6). The two deficits (headache, pain) were initially mis-classified as FLAT because their ASD-vs-NT elevation is ~0. That was wrong — their e_ASD ≈ 0 is the finding (ASD fails to join the spectrum-wide rise). The classifier now checks δ-survival first, so a surviving deficit lands in STEP-DOWN regardless of its NT elevation. Point estimates and S5 are unchanged — only the labeling.

S6–S8-specific caveats (in addition to Part VI): - Ordering is an assumption. NT < other-NDD < ADHD < ASD is clinically reasonable but imposed; every table carries the four raw prevalences so the actual shape is inspectable. - Empirical-Bayes shrinkage is plug-in. DerSimonian–Laird treats τ² as known, making intervals mildly anticonservative — well-calibrated screening, not final inference (a full Bayesian model, agenda item M-III, would propagate τ² uncertainty). - The ascertainment ceiling still applies to the positive findings. Utilization is not divided out of S5–S7. S8's UTIL-NORM shows a cell-by-cell divide-out is possible but fragile; agenda item M-I would build it as a full marginal de-confounder. Until then the two deficits (which run against the more-coding direction) and the AUTISM-SPECIFIC epilepsy signal (large, mechanistically expected) are the most confound-robust results.
